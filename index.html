About St. Thomas Aquinas Seminary

Seminary History

St. Thomas Aquinas Seminary was established to provide a home for the theological and priestly formation of candidates for the priesthood in Kenya. From its early years the seminary has grown into a community of formation, study and prayer, accompanying seminarians through philosophy, theology and pastoral ministry.

Today the seminary serves several dioceses and religious congregations, forming men for ordained ministry in a rapidly changing society while remaining rooted in the unchanging tradition of the Church.

Motto

“Vile Baba Alivyonituma, Nina Watuma Ninyi” — “As the Father has sent me, so I send you” (John 20:21).

Mission

To form priests who are humanly mature, spiritually grounded, intellectually competent and pastorally available — men who will serve the Church and the people of God with humility, integrity and compassion.

Vision

To be a leading centre of theological and priestly formation in Kenya and the wider region, raising servant-leaders who transform their communities and witness to the Gospel in the modern world.

Values of the Institution

  • Faith — rooted in prayer, Scripture and the sacraments.
  • Excellence — in academic study, formation and pastoral work.
  • Community — living and growing together in fraternal charity.
  • Service — availability to the Church and to society.
  • Integrity — honesty, accountability and respect for every person.
  • Communion — fidelity to the Bishop and to the universal Church.

Governance & Accreditation

Canonical Status

An interdiocesan seminary erected by the Catholic Bishops of Kenya, governed by a Board of Trustees and a Rector's Council. The competent authority is the Episcopal Conference.

Academic Affiliation

Programmes are affiliated to a pontifical faculty of theology, with civil recognition through the Catholic University of Eastern Africa, Kenya.

Formation Team

The Rector, Dean of Studies, Dean of Students, Spiritual Director, and appointed formators constitute the formation team, meeting weekly.

Board of Trustees

The Trustees oversee governance, finance and strategic direction on behalf of the sponsoring dioceses.

Formation

Priestly formation at St. Thomas Aquinas Seminary follows the four pillars set out by Pope St. John Paul II — human, spiritual, intellectual and pastoral — integrated into one coherent journey of discipleship.

01

Human Formation

The foundation of all formation. Growth in maturity, freedom, affectivity and the capacity for healthy relationships and self-giving.

02

Spiritual Formation

Daily Eucharist, the Liturgy of the Hours, adoration, confession and regular spiritual direction, leading to a life of union with Christ.

03

Intellectual Formation

Philosophy and theology studied in fidelity to the Church's magisterium, forming pastors capable of preaching and teaching with clarity.

04

Pastoral Formation

Supervised apostolates in parishes, hospitals, schools and prisons, preparing men for the concrete needs of the people they will serve.

Discerning the priesthood?

Admissions

If you sense a call to the priesthood, the first step is to speak with your parish priest or the vocation director of your diocese or religious congregation. The seminary receives candidates sponsored by a diocese or an approved religious institute.

Entry Requirements

  • A confirmed Catholic male, normally between 18 and 35 years of age.
  • Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE) with a minimum mean grade of C+ and a pass in Mathematics and English, or equivalent.
  • Sponsorship by a diocese or religious congregation, evidenced by a letter from the Ordinary or Major Superior.
  • Recommendation from the parish priest and, where applicable, the school.
  • Baptism, Confirmation and First Holy Communion certificates.
  • Medical and psychological fitness attested by qualified professionals.
  • Good conduct certificate and, where required, police clearance.

How to Apply

  1. Speak to your parish priest. Begin spiritual accompaniment and vocational discernment in your home parish.
  2. Contact your diocesan vocation director. Applications normally pass through the diocese, not directly to the seminary.
  3. Gather your documents. Certificates, recommendations, medical and psychological reports.
  4. Complete the application form. Available from the diocesan vocation office or the seminary registrar.
  5. Attend an interview and assessment. The formation team meets shortlisted candidates and their sponsors.
  6. Receive a decision. Successful applicants are notified in writing and invited to join at the start of the academic year.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does formation take?

Normally eight years in total: one year of spiritual formation and pre-philosophy, three years of philosophy, one year of pastoral internship, and four years of theology, followed by diaconate and priestly ordination.

Who pays for my formation?

Formation is normally sponsored by your diocese or religious congregation, supported by the generosity of the faithful. Personal contributions may be requested where the sponsor is not able to cover the full cost.

Can I apply directly to the seminary?

No. Applications pass through a diocese or religious institute, which sponsors the candidate. The seminary can help you make contact with the right office.

What if I am not sure yet?

That is normal. Speak with your parish priest, join a Come & See weekend, and take time in prayer and spiritual direction. Discernment is a process, not a single decision.

Do you accept candidates from outside Kenya?

Yes, subject to sponsorship by a diocese or religious institute and to immigration requirements. Contact the Registrar for details.

Community

Study

Academics

A rigorous programme in philosophy and theology, taught in fidelity to the Church's teaching and oriented towards pastoral service.

Philosophy Programme

Three years covering logic, metaphysics, epistemology, ethics, philosophy of God, anthropology and the history of philosophy, with a strong emphasis on St. Thomas Aquinas.

Theology Programme

Four years covering Scripture, dogmatic and moral theology, sacraments, liturgy, canon law, Church history, and pastoral theology.

Pastoral Internship

A supervised year of pastoral placement in a parish or institution between philosophy and theology, with regular reflection and reporting.

Protecting the vulnerable

Safeguarding

The Catholic Church in Kenya is committed to the protection of children and vulnerable adults. St. Thomas Aquinas Seminary operates a zero-tolerance policy towards abuse in any form.

Our Commitment

  • All formators, staff and volunteers are vetted and reference-checked.
  • Safeguarding training is mandatory and renewed annually.
  • A named Safeguarding Officer is available to receive concerns.
  • All concerns are reported to the relevant civil and ecclesiastical authorities.
  • Survivors are offered pastoral support and referrals to professional services.

Report a Concern

If you have a concern about the safety or welfare of a child or vulnerable adult, please contact us immediately. You may also report directly to the police.
